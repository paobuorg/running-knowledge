## Classic does not mean unchanged

Boston, Berlin, London, New York, Tokyo and Chicago, along with trail races such as UTMB, Western States and Hardrock, are often called classics. They have long histories, a stable culture and a large public record, but courses, start and finish, entry rules and race dates change. The Boston Marathon course and qualifying standards are updated by year and category; trail routes may change because of weather, permits or safety.

When reading a classic race, first establish which edition, category, course and rule set you are looking at. Comparing courses and times across years without that context easily produces the wrong conclusion.

## What history adds

History explains tradition, difficulty and cultural position: why a climb becomes a landmark, why an event has a particular entry standard, why a course is known as fast or hard. It also shows that today’s route is the result of long evolution, not a natural fact.

History does not replace current information. Entry, ballot, cut-offs, aid, transport and mandatory kit follow the organiser’s latest notice; an old course map is not current navigation.

## Times and conditions

Weather, wind, temperature and field density differ between editions. Records require event rules and course measurement, and recreational times are also affected by the day’s conditions. When comparing, state the race, year, category and course version; across races, also consider distance, climb, surface and altitude.

A famous race is not automatically right for everyone. Choose by training base, travel cost, course difficulty, cut-offs and recovery, not by reputation alone.

## Using this site’s course files

Each route page is tied to a fixed version and lists distance, elevation method, direction, anchor and sources. We do not draw a simulated route when reliable geometry is unavailable; race names, marks and official text remain with the organiser. Read the version and sources first, then the sections and training notes; for entry or navigation, return to the organiser’s official page.

## Scope and limits

This page does not provide entry, eligibility, route access or result ratification advice, or judge whether a race suits you. Rules and routes change; the organiser, World Athletics and local land manager’s latest information govern.

## Sources and scope

Marathon distance and event definition follow World Athletics; record and result-ratification rules follow the World Athletics records page; Boston Marathon entry and competition rules follow the BAA’s official rules document.
