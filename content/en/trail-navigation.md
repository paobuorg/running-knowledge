## Plan before leaving

Trail navigation starts before the trailhead. Choose the route, direction, expected time and turnaround point; check the weather, sunrise and sunset, snow, rain and flash-flood risk; and leave a trip plan with a trusted person who is not coming along, including the route, companions, departure and return times. The NPS Hike Smart guidance recommends leaving a plan with a trusted person and preparing a Plan B.

Do not rely on a phone alone. Cold, a fall, water or a dead battery can take it out of the equation; carry a paper map, compass, offline GPX, headlamp and backup power according to the route and conditions. A GPS watch is an aid, not the only navigation tool.

## What a GPX can and cannot do

A GPX records coordinates, elevation and waypoints. It can confirm direction at a junction and show approximate distance and climb. It does not contain live closures, broken bridges, snow depth, water availability, access permission or rescue access. A track may be old, badly recorded or redistributed without permission; a line on a map is not proof that a route is open.

Load the GPX into an offline map before leaving and check key turns, aid points and escape points. Print a simplified map or write down key coordinates as a backup when electronics fail. Treat the GPX as a planning base and the land manager or race organiser’s latest notice as the authority.

## On the trail

Keep reading the terrain, not just the watch. Stop at junctions to check the map and direction; if you are off route, return to the last certain point instead of cutting across. Agree on meeting points and turnaround times within the group; if energy, weather or time is worse than expected, shortening the route is safer than “just seeing what is around the next corner”.

Phone coverage may be absent. The NPS notes that mobile coverage can be unreliable and suggests considering a locator beacon and conserving battery. At night or in bad weather, a headlamp, whistle, extra warmth and first-aid kit are not decorations; carry what the route, season and local rules require.

## Retreat and rescue

Decide in advance what would make you turn around: late time, worsening weather, injury, running out of food or water, or a route blocked by water or snow. Retreat is part of navigation, not a failure. Tell your companions and contact about the decision; do not enter unknown terrain just to complete a track.

When calling for help, give location, number of people, injuries, weather and remaining supplies. If there is no signal, use the planned beacon or move to a known coverage point. Do not leave an injured person alone in dangerous terrain.

## Scope and limits

This page does not grant access, provide live conditions or guarantee rescue. Private land, protected areas and race routes have their own rules; check the land manager, organiser and weather service before leaving, and get the navigation and first-aid training the route demands.
