## Pain is information, not a willpower test

When pain appears during a run, stop or reduce intensity and observe whether it worsens with continued running, changes your gait, or comes with swelling, weakness or a joint that catches. The NHS explicitly advises not running while in pain and returning gradually after recovery. Pushing through pain does not automatically become safe training through “adaptation”.

## Record, do not guess a diagnosis

Record the location, when it began, the movement that brings it on (uphill, downhill, acceleration, first steps after sitting), duration and recent training load. Turning “knee pain” into specific timing and movement information helps a clinician or physiotherapist. This page does not diagnose by body part or treat a common label as a confirmed cause.

## Load change is not the only cause

A systematic review of 36 studies and 23,047 runners reported that about 26.2% had a running-related injury. Evidence linking weekly distance, duration, frequency, intensity or recent changes to injury was conflicting. The authors explicitly caution against one “optimal” progression for every runner. Load matters, but it does not explain every pain.

## When to seek help

NHS advice includes urgent assessment for severe knee pain or swelling; review by a GP or physiotherapist if knee pain persists beyond a week, or for Achilles pain lasting two to three weeks; sudden sharp Achilles pain may be a tear and should be assessed promptly. Other reasons not to wait include inability to bear weight, obvious deformity, fever, pain waking you at night, or persistent palpitations, chest pain or fainting. Use local emergency services for emergencies.

## Relative rest, not total stillness

Rest does not mean doing nothing. Temporarily reduce running volume and avoid the speeds and gradients that provoke pain, while keeping comfortable low-intensity movement. When symptoms settle, test with short, easy run-walk intervals and build gradually. The NHS progression suggestion is to repeat a similar run at least three or four times before increasing distance or pace. A clinician who knows your situation should adjust the plan.

## Scope and limits

This page does not diagnose, recommend medication, replace imaging or a physical examination, or promise that “form, shoes or stretching” will cure pain. Causes may involve previous injury, bone stress, tendons, muscles or other medical issues. Do not delay professional assessment when it is needed.

## Sources and scope
