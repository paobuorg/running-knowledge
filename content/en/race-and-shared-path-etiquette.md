## Race etiquette starts with rules

The Boston Marathon rules state that race bibs are non-transferable; participants may not run with anyone who is not registered as an official participant on any part of the course; portable music devices may be used with headphones only if participants maintain awareness of their surroundings, with stricter rules for professional, wheelchair and para divisions; and all participants must follow the event code of conduct and competition rules.

These are entry conditions, not suggestions. Bib swapping, unofficial pacing, bypassing check-in, moving against the flow at aid stations or cutting across the course can affect others’ safety and lead to disqualification. Rules differ between events; read the current race manual before entering.

## Start, passing and aid stations

Start in your assigned corral and do not weave sideways or stop for a photo without warning. Pass on the left or the direction specified by the race, and give space early. When you hear footsteps or “on your left”, do not change line suddenly. Signal before entering an aid station, take what you need, then move forward before slowing; do not stop at the table.

Do not throw litter on the course or roadside; use the designated zones. If you need to walk, move to the side rather than stopping in the middle. Predictable movement reduces collisions and trips.

## Shared paths

On pavements, greenways and park paths, keep to your side, give a verbal warning before passing, do not take the full width with a group, and slow down around children, older people, dogs and cyclists. Specific right-of-way rules vary by city, road and site; follow signs and local law.

At night, make yourself visible and slow down at corners and blind spots; do not step into the road from behind parked cars or obstructions. On trails, follow the land manager’s rules, do not cross closures or cut switchbacks, and do not disturb wildlife. Race routes and everyday access can differ.

## Conflict and safety

If you are harassed, followed or involved in a collision, move to a lit, populated place with people or cameras and contact local police or emergency services; do not stop to confront someone to “win” the moment. In a race, move to the side and seek medical help if unwell rather than collapsing in the middle of the course.

## Scope and limits

This page is not the full race rulebook, traffic law or land-management rule. The race manual and technical meeting govern a race; local law, signs and the land manager govern everyday routes. Rules change, so check the date when citing them.

## Sources and scope

Non-transferable bibs, the ban on unregistered pacers, headphone conditions and the requirement to follow the event code of conduct follow the BAA 2026 Boston Marathon rules document; shared-path and trail access rules must be checked with the local authority.
