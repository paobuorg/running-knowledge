## Scope first

A glossary is a reading tool, not an exam answer. The same word can have different measurement conventions across coaches, studies and devices: the intensity of a “tempo” run, a VO2max protocol and a GPS distance algorithm may all differ. Treat a definition as a starting point, and check the basis, units and test conditions before comparing numbers.

## Pace and speed

- **Pace**: time per unit distance, usually min/km or min/mi. A smaller pace number means a faster speed.
- **Speed**: distance per unit time, usually km/h or m/s. Speed and pace are reciprocal relationships, but the units differ.
- **Average pace**: total time divided by total distance; stops, hills and changes are averaged out and do not describe every segment.

## Effort and session types

- **RPE (rating of perceived exertion)**: a number for how hard the effort feels, often on a 0–10 or 6–20 scale. It is personal and best cross-checked with the talk test and heart-rate trends.
- **Easy run**: a low-intensity run where full sentences are possible and you finish with something left; there is no single pace.
- **Tempo run**: a sustained “comfortably hard” session, often near but not beyond a threshold; exact intensity and duration vary by plan.
- **Intervals**: repeated higher-quality work bouts with recovery between them; work and recovery can be defined by time or distance.
- **Long run**: the longer easy or endurance run of the week, used for sustained movement, fuelling and route practice, not a weekly maximum test.
- **Taper**: a planned reduction in training before a race, often retaining some intensity or race feel; there is no universal number of days.

## Technique terms

- **Cadence**: steps per minute. It is not a fixed 180; it changes with speed, gradient, height and fatigue.
- **Stride length**: distance covered per step, usually increasing with speed; longer is not automatically better.
- **Elevation gain**: the sum of all positive elevation changes, not the finish minus the start.
- **Grade**: elevation change divided by horizontal distance, often expressed as a percentage; uphill and downhill should be considered separately.

## Physiology and training concepts

- **VO2max**: a marker of the upper limit of oxygen use during high-intensity exercise; laboratory and watch estimates use different methods and are not interchangeable.
- **Lactate threshold**: the intensity at which blood lactate begins to accumulate markedly; definitions and testing methods are not unique, so one number cannot capture all training.
- **Running economy**: the oxygen cost of running at a given speed; speed, surface, shoes, fatigue and individual variation matter, so comparisons need controlled conditions.

## File formats

- **GPX**: an XML GPS data exchange format that can contain track points, route points, time and optional elevation; not every GPX contains the same fields.
- **GeoJSON**: a JSON geospatial format whose coordinates are usually longitude, latitude and an optional third elevation value; LineString and MultiLineString are common line geometries in track tools.

## How to use the terms

When reading training content, check whether it means pace, speed or perceived effort. In a route profile, distinguish cumulative climb, net change and grade. In a tool result, confirm units, distance basis and whether elevation is present. If unsure, return to the source and its conditions rather than memorising a number.

## Scope and limits

This page does not provide an individual training prescription, medical diagnosis or guarantee of device accuracy. VO2max and lactate-threshold numbers require qualified testing and interpretation; pain or disease management should involve the relevant professional.
