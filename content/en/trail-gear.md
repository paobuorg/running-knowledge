## Start with the route and weather

The NPS Ten Essentials list navigation, sun protection, clothing, illumination, first aid, fire, repair kit, food, water and water treatment, and shelter as a minimum framework. It is not a mandatory kit for every race, but it is a reminder that gear should cover getting lost, bad weather, injury or darkness — not only a smooth finish.

Before a race, read the organiser's mandatory kit and weather plan. For training, decide according to distance, elevation, aid stations, phone coverage and how hard evacuation would be. A short, familiar city path and a remote mountain trail do not need the same equipment.

## Headlamp and navigation

NPS recommends a headlamp with spare batteries, plus a map and compass or GPS; do not rely on a phone, because mountain areas often have no signal. Test the headlamp's brightness, runtime and fit before race day, and keep spare batteries within easy reach.

Navigation files can also be wrong: a GPX is route data, not live conditions. Check the track version, key junctions and escape points before starting, and carry a paper or offline map as backup. Do not simply follow someone else's footprints.

## Pack, water and food

Fit and access matter more than capacity at first. A pack that is too large encourages unnecessary weight; one that is too small cannot hold a warm layer, water and first-aid kit. Plan water around aid stations, weather and sweat rate; for long or remote routes, carry a water-treatment option.

Pack one more portion of food than planned, especially when delays or navigation errors are possible. Keep the first-aid kit, emergency blanket, whistle and headlamp where you can reach them without unpacking everything.

## Clothing, chafing and checks

Trail running involves rain, wind, brush and hours of friction. Layer clothing for the forecast, use anti-chafe measures where needed, and choose socks, gloves and a waterproof shell that work together. Test every item in training; do not wear new shoes, a new pack or a new rain jacket for the first time on race day.

After long runs, check shoe soles, pack zips, headlamp charge and clothing tears; replace worn or failed items. Lightweight should never come at the cost of illumination, warmth or navigation.

## What this page does not provide

This page does not recommend specific brands or replace a race's mandatory-kit list and route description. Technical terrain, glaciers, avalanche terrain or roped routes require specific training and local guidance; do not enter terrain beyond your ability on the strength of one article.

## Sources and scope

The minimum kit framework follows the NPS Ten Essentials; headlamp, navigation, water and return-plan guidance follows NPS hiking-safety advice; traction and warmth in snow and ice follow NPS winter guidance. Actual needs depend on race rules, land-manager guidance and the forecast.
