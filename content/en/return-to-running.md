## First identify what you are returning from

The starting point might be a cold, travel, a busy period, a pain episode or a long layoff. Different causes need different conditions: do not resume running while pain persists, walking is abnormal, swelling is present or pain wakes you at night; seek medical or physiotherapy assessment first. The NHS explicitly advises not running while in pain.

## Start with walking and run-walk

If everyday walking is pain-free and stable, use short run-walk intervals for the first week: brief running, generous walking recovery and an easy total time. NHS Couch to 5K also starts with one-minute runs and 1.5-minute walks, three times a week with rest between. Adjust the ratio to your response.

## Repeat the same dose several times

NHS progression advice is to repeat a similar run at least three or four times before increasing distance or pace. The longer the break, the more those repeats matter; do not add more just because one session felt good. Record the rest of the day and the next day: pain, fatigue, sleep and normal walking.

## When to pause

If running worsens pain, changes your gait, causes new swelling or persists into the next day, stop and return to pain-free activity; seek professional assessment if symptoms persist or recur. Fever, chest pain, fainting or unusual breathlessness are not things to “adapt to”; stop and seek medical help.

## Scope and limits

This page does not give an individual return-to-running plan or diagnose an injury, and does not promise that a progression suits you. Post-surgical recovery, bone-stress injury, cardiovascular conditions and postpartum return need guidance from the relevant professional, not a web page alone.

## Sources and scope

Pain warnings and progression follow the NHS running-injury page; the run-walk structure follows NHS Couch to 5K. This is a general recovery framework, not a medical prescription.
